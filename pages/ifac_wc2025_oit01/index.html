Powered Transfemoral/Transtibial Prostheses

IFAC World Congress 2026 — Open Invited Track

Powered lower-limb prostheses are transitioning from laboratory prototypes to clinically viable devices. This OIT convenes the control, robotics, rehabilitation, and AI communities to accelerate robust powered knee/ankle solutions for transfemoral and transtibial amputees, spanning control & estimation, intent-awareness, personalization, hardware, and clinical translation.

Call for Papers Explore Topics

Why this OIT now?

  • Clinical need & quality of life. Individuals with transfemoral and transtibial amputations face elevated metabolic cost, fall risk, and limited mobility. Powered devices can inject positive work, reduce compensations, and improve stability on stairs, ramps, and uneven terrain.
  • Convergence of technologies. High torque–density actuators, compact batteries, high-rate sensing, and on-device compute enable real-time, adaptive policies that are safer and more versatile.
  • Translation gap. Real-world robustness, personalization, and standardized evaluation remain open challenges; adoption requires clinically explainable control and validated outcomes.

Keywords: Powered prosthesis; transfemoral; transtibial; powered knee–ankle; phase-variable control; variable impedance; intent recognition (EMG, inertial, vision); personalization & human-in-the-loop optimization; RL; digital twins; safety & certification; open datasets; clinical trials; benchmarking.

Primary IFAC TC: 8.2 Biological and Medical Systems · Co-review requested: 4.3 Robotics

Focus Areas

  • Model- & data-driven control
  • Phase-variable & intent-aware estimation
  • User-specific personalization & co-adaptation
  • Actuation, sensing, & energy management
  • Clinical translation & benchmarking

Topics of Interest

  • Control & Estimation

    • Autonomous phase-variable control for knees/ankles; variable impedance & hybrid strategies
    • MPC, CLF–CBF safety filters; socket/ground interaction observers
    • Learning-in-the-loop: preference learning, policy adaptation, meta-/few-shot tuning

  • Intent & Environment Awareness

    • EMG-driven intent, IMU/load-cell fusion, adaptive state machines
    • Exteroceptive perception (vision/lidar) for stairs, ramps, uneven terrain
    • Uncertainty-aware estimation and safety constraints

  • Personalization

    • Clinically explainable tuning protocols and guardrails
    • Onboard learning and preference-aware optimization
    • Generalization across subjects, tasks, and daily-living scenarios

  • Hardware & Sensing

    • High torque-density actuation (QDD, SEA/VSA); energy regeneration
    • Compact, reliable socket & limb sensing; battery/thermal management
    • Co-design to reduce mass without sacrificing torque bandwidth

  • Clinical Translation

    • Outcome metrics and multi-center trials; failures and field data
    • Verification & certification aligned with medical-device standards
    • Rehabilitation integration and real-world deployment reports

  • Open Data & Benchmarks

    • Open designs and datasets for reproducibility
    • Standardized evaluation protocols and prosthesis emulators
    • Comparative studies: powered vs. passive/microprocessor devices
